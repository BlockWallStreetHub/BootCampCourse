02 — Lending Account Manager

Lending Account Manager

DeFi lending lets you borrow against your crypto instead of selling it. You lock up collateral (say ETH or BTC), borrow a stablecoin against it, and keep your upside. The price you pay is that a big enough drop in your collateral can get you liquidated: a bot repays part of your debt and takes your collateral, plus a bonus for itself. The Lending Account Manager exists so that never surprises you.

The one number to know: health factor

Health factor = (collateral value × liquidation threshold) ÷ debt value

If the health factor drops below 1.0, your position can be liquidated. Example: $10,000 of collateral with an 80% liquidation threshold and $5,000 of debt gives (10,000 × 0.80) ÷ 5,000 = 1.6. Your collateral could lose a good chunk of its value before you reach 1.0, but not forever, and fast crashes happen.

What the tool does

  • Watches Aave and Instadapp accounts. Add a wallet address, or an Instadapp smart account (DSA) address, and choose the network: Ethereum, Arbitrum, Base, Optimism or Avalanche.
  • Shows health in plain colors: good, caution, danger. It also graphs collateral against debt.
  • More lending accounts panel: Morpho (several networks), Kamino (Solana) and Jupiter Lend (Solana). Jupiter needs a free API key that you paste in, and its health figure is an estimate, so check it against Jupiter's own app. Fluid currently gives you a link to check on fluid.io.
  • Alerts you set yourself. You pick the floor and how you want to be told (on screen, and by email only for alerts you choose). It rechecks accounts about every 90 seconds while the page is open.
  • Liquidation record. Press Scan the past to pull earlier liquidations for the accounts you watch (7 days up to 1 year), see what was seized, what debt was repaid and the penalty, and export to CSV.

Your three levers when health gets low

  1. Repay some debt. Raises health directly.
  2. Add collateral. Same effect from the other side.
  3. Swap collateral into a stablecoin (or swap the debt) so a falling price stops hurting you. Platforms such as Instadapp and DeFi Saver offer these swaps.
Set two lines, not one

The Desk's big-alert line is 1.02, the last call before liquidation. That is too late to be your only alert. Add a softer early-warning line well above it, so you have hours or days to act, not minutes. Where exactly is your call and depends on how volatile your collateral is. The more volatile, the more room you want.

Watch out

Borrowing more against your collateral lowers your health factor. A falling price on volatile collateral can move you from "fine" to "liquidated" faster than you can open the app.

03 — LP Scout

LP Scout

A liquidity pool (LP) is a pot of two tokens that traders swap against. If you deposit both, you earn a share of the trading fees. LP Scout searches pools on every chain, lets you filter by size and yield, and then simulates how a pool could play out for you.

Where the money comes from

Fee APR ≈ (24h volume × fee tier × 365) ÷ TVL

Example: a pool with $10M of TVL, $2M of daily volume and a 0.05% fee tier earns $2,000,000 × 0.0005 = $1,000 a day in fees. Over a year that is $365,000 ÷ $10M = 3.65% for a full-range provider. The key idea is volume relative to TVL. A big pool with little trading earns little.

Concentrated liquidity (a tight price range) multiplies fee earnings while price stays inside the range, but you earn nothing when price leaves it. A full range earns less but never goes out of range.

The three costs people forget

  • Impermanent loss (IL). When the two tokens move apart, you end up with less than if you had just held them. If one token doubles against the other, IL is about 5.7%. At 4× it is 20%. Fees have to earn that back.
  • Reward tokens. A huge APR is often mostly reward tokens on top of fees. Those rewards can drop in price, and they are not trading fees.
  • Thin pools. Tiny TVL can make an APR look enormous and make it hard to get out.
Measure in the asset you want more of

If your goal is to end with more ETH or more BTC, judge the position in ETH or BTC, not just dollars. Dollar profit can hide a loss in ETH terms when ETH outruns the other token.

What the tool does

  • Searches pools by chain, and filters by minimum size and yield.
  • Has a built-in simulator for any pool you pick. It runs 1,000 random price paths and shows the range of possible outcomes, not one lucky number.
  • Lets you choose how often fees are compounded (never, daily, weekly and so on).
  • Has a filter for Robinhood Chain stock pools: only the pools holding stock tokens such as NVDA, AAPL and TSLA.
Watch out

The simulator uses random paths, so it shows what could happen, not what will. Read the bad outcomes first.

Do it on the Desk

Filter for a minimum pool size so you skip tiny pools.

Sort by yield, then check volume against TVL for the top few.

Open one pool and run the simulator. Change how often fees compound.

Write down one pool you would watch, and why.

04 — LP Position Manager

LP Position Manager

LP Scout helps you find a pool. The LP Position Manager watches the pools you are already in. Paste your public wallet address and it reads your positions straight from the blockchain, with no sign-in and no wallet connection.

What "range" means

In a concentrated pool you choose a price range. While price is inside it, you earn fees. If price leaves, you earn nothing, and you are left holding only one side:

  • Price falls below your range: you hold 100% of the coin that fell.
  • Price rises above your range: you hold 100% of the other coin (you sold your upside along the way).

For every position the tool shows the range, the exact prices where you go out of range, and what you would be holding if you did.

The Hold / Rebalance / Close meter

The meter reads market conditions, using the 7-day and 30-day trend, how choppy the last two weeks were, and the odds price walks back into your range within 14 days. Then it leans one way:

MarketThe meter leansWhy
Strongly bearish coinCloseFalling price plus impermanent loss compound each other
Calm, sideways or risingHoldFees keep coming and price often drifts back
Slightly out of range, good odds of returningHold / waitRebalancing costs gas and locks in the loss you are sitting on
Out of range, comeback unlikelyRebalanceIt also suggests a new range
Why waiting is often right

Rebalancing is not free. You pay gas, and by moving the position you lock in the loss you are currently carrying. If price is likely to come back, patience can beat activity.

Coverage and limits

  • Reads Uniswap, PancakeSwap, SushiSwap, Aerodrome and Velodrome positions automatically.
  • For Raydium, Orca, Meteora and others, use Add by hand: paste the pool address, press Auto-fill, then type your range low and high from the DEX screen.
  • Staked positions (in gauges or farms) also need adding by hand, because the position NFT sits in the farm contract rather than your wallet.
  • Optional refresh every 2 minutes and notifications when a position leaves or re-enters its range (the tab has to stay open).
Watch out

The meter reads past prices only. It can't see news, hacks or listings. It is a rules-based signal for education, not advice, and its impermanent-loss figures leave out the fees you have earned.

05 — EMA Backtester

EMA Backtester

An EMA (exponential moving average) is an average of recent prices that gives more weight to the newest prices, so it reacts faster than a simple average. The Desk focuses on four lines traders watch constantly: the 20, 50, 100 and 200 EMA.

EMA multiplier = 2 ÷ (N + 1) → for a 20 EMA: 2 ÷ 21 ≈ 0.095

How people use them

  • Price above a rising EMA = uptrend. Price below a falling EMA = downtrend.
  • A 20 EMA shows short-term momentum. A 200 EMA shows the long-term trend.
  • Crossovers: price crossing an EMA, or a fast EMA crossing a slow one, is treated as a buy or sell signal.
  • Timeframe changes everything. On daily candles you get more signals and more noise. On weekly and monthly you get fewer, slower, bigger signals.
The catch

EMAs lag. They tell you what already happened, so in choppy sideways markets they give false signals (called whipsaws). A rule that works beautifully in one year can fail the next. That is exactly why we backtest.

What the tool does

  • Enter a ticker or contract address, the amount you would have invested, and a timeframe (daily, weekly or monthly), and see how buying and selling on an EMA would have played out.
  • The chart marks gold stars for buys and blue moons for sells.
  • Rule Matrix tests every EMA and timeframe combination against plain buy and hold, with a sortable table, a heatmap, an equity curve and the individual trades for each rule. Back-tests can go up to 15 years where the data exists.
  • Strategy Maker projects 90, 180 or 365 days ahead by reshuffling blocks of real historical returns. It gives you a range of possible outcomes, not a prediction.

How not to fool yourself

  • Beat buy and hold or don't bother. A strategy that makes money but earns less than just holding, with more work, is a loss.
  • The best row in a big table is partly luck. Test dozens of rules and one will look great by chance. Re-test the winner on a second coin and a second date range.
  • Real trading has fees and slippage that a clean back-test may not include.
  • Past results do not promise future results.

Do it on the Desk

Open the EMA Backtester and enter BTC, then ETH.

Run the Rule Matrix. Sort by return, then check how each rule did against buy and hold.

Open the best rule's trades. Look for the stars and moons on the chart.

Run the same rule on a different coin and a different period. Does it hold up?

Try Strategy Maker and read the spread of outcomes, not just the middle.

06 — Moon Desk

Moon Desk

Traders have argued for generations about the moon, the zodiac and eclipses and the markets. Some swear by it. Many roll their eyes. The Moon Desk takes neither side. It lets you lay moon phases, zodiac signs and eclipses behind real price charts and then test the idea with a back-test, so the data does the arguing.

What the tool does

  • Shows real price charts as candlesticks on daily, weekly and monthly views you can zoom and pan, with moon phases, zodiac signs and eclipses drawn behind them.
  • Runs a back-test of moon-based rules (for example buying around one phase and selling around another) and compares the result with buy and hold.
  • Checks your rule against many fake calendars, which is the part that matters most (see below).

The real lesson: is it signal or noise?

Any pattern can look real in hindsight. The honest question is: would random dates do just as well? If a rule built on the moon only matches what random calendars produce, you have found noise. If it consistently beats them, it deserves a closer look, and even then a second asset and a second period.

Read the fine print

• The fake calendars in a test can sit only days apart and overlap a lot, so a strong-looking result can be stronger than it really is.
• Moon phases use the average lunar cycle, so a phase edge can be off by about half a day.
• A pattern that fits the past is not a promise about the future.

You don't have to believe in astrology to learn from this tool. The skill it trains is the one that protects your money everywhere else: test the story before you trade the story.

Note

The basic back-test is open to everyone. The Moon Desk's extra member features (the AI "Ask" box, the Strategist schedule and saved settings) are locked to the owner code, so you won't see them unlocked in class.

Do it on the Desk

Open the Moon Desk and pick BTC.

Switch between daily, weekly and monthly charts and find a recent eclipse.

Run a back-test with a rule such as "buy new moon, sell full moon".

Compare the result with buy and hold, and read what it says about the random calendars.

Repeat on ETH. Did the same rule behave the same way?

07 — Putting It Together

Putting It Together

Five tools, one workflow. Here is how a disciplined user can run the Desk in about ten minutes a day.

Your daily Desk routine

Protect first. Open the Lending Account Manager. Is any health factor near your early-warning line? Fix that before anything else.

Check your pools. Open the LP Position Manager. Did anything leave its range? Read the meter and the reasons.

Hunt carefully. Only then look at LP Scout for new pools. Check volume against TVL, and where the yield comes from.

Test before you trade. Have an idea? Run it through the EMA Backtester, and the Moon Desk if it is calendar-based. Beat buy and hold on more than one coin, or skip it.

Write it down. Note what you decided and why. A decision journal beats memory.

09 — Glossary

Vocabulary from today.

Health Factor
A single number showing how far a loan sits from liquidation: (collateral value × liquidation threshold) ÷ debt value. Below 1.0 the position can be liquidated.
Liquidation
When a lending protocol lets a liquidator repay part of your debt and take your collateral, plus a bonus, because the loan became unsafe.
Liquidation Threshold
The share of your collateral's value that counts toward keeping the loan safe. It is the number you multiply by in the health factor formula.
Liquidity Pool (LP)
A pot of two tokens that traders swap against. Providers deposit both and earn a share of the trading fees.
TVL (Total Value Locked)
The total dollar value deposited in a pool or protocol.
Fee APR
Estimated yearly return from trading fees alone: (24h volume × fee tier × 365) ÷ TVL.
Concentrated Liquidity
Providing liquidity inside a chosen price range. It earns more fees per dollar while price is inside the range and nothing when price leaves.
Impermanent Loss
The difference between holding a token pair in a pool and simply holding the two tokens. A 2× relative move costs about 5.7%.
Out of Range
When price has moved beyond a position's range, so it holds one token only and earns no fees.
EMA (Exponential Moving Average)
An average of recent prices that weights the newest prices more, so it reacts faster than a simple average.
Back-test
Running a trading rule over past prices to see how it would have performed. It shows what happened, not what will happen.
Buy and Hold
The benchmark every strategy has to beat: buying the asset and doing nothing.
Whipsaw
A false signal in a choppy market, where a moving-average rule buys and sells repeatedly for small losses.
Overfitting
Tuning a rule until it fits past data perfectly, often by luck, so it fails on new data.
